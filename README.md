The problem: Right now, if you're a marine scientist or fisheries officer in India, the information you need is scattered. Ocean temperature data sits with one agency. Fish catch records sit with another. Species and DNA data sits somewhere else entirely. Nobody has a single place to look and ask "what's going on in this area, across all of this?"

What we're building: One platform that pulls all three types of data together onto a live map, and lets you talk to it like you'd talk to a knowledgeable colleague.

Concretely, three things it lets you do:

Look at a map and see everything at once — ocean buoys, fish catch zones, coral reef health, species sightings — all as dots and layers you can click into.
Ask it questions in plain English — instead of digging through spreadsheets, you type "why is sardine catch dropping near Kerala?" and it actually looks up the real ocean temperature data, the real catch numbers, and gives you a grounded answer with sources — not a guess.
See warnings before problems get bad — instead of just showing you what already happened, it predicts things: "this coral reef is at high risk of bleaching in the next few weeks," or "sardine stock is likely to keep declining for 3 more months." That's the difference between reacting after a crisis and catching it early.

Who this actually helps: fisheries departments deciding when to issue advisories, conservation teams deciding which reefs to check first, and researchers who currently waste time hunting across five different databases just to answer one question.

The honest version for your pitch: the map and data structure are real, the AI question-answering is real (using an actual model, actual retrieval), and the predictions are simple-but-legitimate models — nothing here is smoke and mirrors, it's just built fast and scoped small on purpose.
